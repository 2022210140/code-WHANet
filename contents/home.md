&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;As a non-invasive, high-resolution fundus imaging technique, OCTA has demonstrated unique advantages in the early diagnosis and dynamic monitoring of fundus diseases such as diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma and choroidal neovascularization. Although a large number of deep learning-based methods have emerged in the field of retinal vascular segmentation in recent years, most of these models are designed for traditional color fundus images, and their performance is very limited on OCTA images.The inherent high-noise interference, low-contrast characteristics, and complex distribution of vascular networks (e.g., tiny bifurcations, pathologically abnormal vascular hyperplasia, etc.) of OCTA images present severe challenges to automated vascular segmentation algorithms. challenges.
To solve the above problems, we propose a two-branch network architecture (WHANet) based on joint spatial-frequency domain optimization, which contains a base skeleton network (UNet), a hybrid attention deep convolutional branch (HADCB) and a multi-scale wavelet feature fusion branch (MWFFB). Two innovative modules are added to each of the two branches: the spatial depth-separated convolutional attention module (SDSCAM), and the multi-frequency feature fusion module (MFFM). With our proposed WHANet, experiments are conducted on the OCTA500 and OCTA-RV datasets in comparison with state-of-the-art methods, and WHANet achieves an overall better level in most of the evaluation metrics. The experimental results show that the completeness and noise immunity of blood vessel segmentation are significantly improved by the joint optimization of spatial-domain attention enhancement and frequency-domain wavelet decomposition.
